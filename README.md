# Poker Night


A new, original play-money Texas Hold'em website for private games with friends. Colourful responsive poker table, dark menus, real multiplayer, cash games and single-table tournaments, three local felt themes, six card backs, avatars, live avatar reactions, chat, hand summaries and table-session statistics.

## Run

Use Node.js 22 or newer. Run `npm ci`, then `npm start`, and open http://localhost:3000. Create a table, copy its invite link, and share it with friends. The host starts the game once at least two players have joined. Use separate browsers or private windows when testing multiple players on one device.

## Deploy on Render

Create a Render Web Service connected to this repository. Runtime: Node. Build command: `npm ci`. Start command: `npm start`. Health path: `/health`. Render sets `PORT` automatically. Alternatively use the included `render.yaml` Blueprint. Use one running instance: game state is held in that server's memory. Do not enable horizontal scaling without adding shared storage and Socket.IO coordination.

Custom defaults: 1,000 chips, 5/10 blinds, rebuy when broke. Tournament defaults: 1,000 chips, 5/10 blinds (100 BB), gentle blind increases every 7 minutes (stack and level duration are configurable), no late entry after starting and no rebuys. Eight seats, 30-second turns; timeout checks when possible and folds otherwise. Every seated player can edit blinds, starting bank and turn timer in Settings. Changes during a hand take effect at the next deal. Starting bank sets lobby stacks before the first deal and future join/rebuy amounts afterwards. Each player has three time cards per table session, adding 60 seconds each to their current turn. Spectators can join a running table, watch public cards and chat; they do not receive private cards or edit settings. Cash spectators can take an open seat for a later hand; tournament seating closes after starting. Appearance is selectable in Collection. Emotes broadcast only to the sender's table. Seated players' reactions appear over their avatars. Spectators can send repeatedly with no emote cooldown; their reactions enter from the left and their emote bank stays open. The client retains at most ten simultaneous spectator videos in two columns of five, replacing the oldest visual during larger bursts.

## Limits

Play money only. Google accounts are available for persistent Cash bankrolls; there are no payments, money deposits or prizes. Invite codes are access links, not authentication. Player reconnect tokens are stored per browser tab session. Custom/Tournament table state and session statistics reset on server restart or redeploy. Cash wallets and settled Cash stacks persist in PostgreSQL. Empty rooms expire after an hour. Free hosting may sleep or restart; use an always-on service for uninterrupted evenings. Tournament blind changes apply between hands. Statistics show measured table-session values, not invented data. Hand history includes per-action replays for recorded hands. Add bot fills an open seat with an automated opponent using the solver and fallback strategy described below. Bots join cash games on the next hand; add tournament bots before the first deal. No multi-table tournaments in this version.

Card shuffling uses Node's cryptographic random integer generator. Betting and cards are managed server-side; each player receives only their own private cards until showdown. This is a friends-only prototype and has not had a security or independent poker-rules audit.


## Bot strategy and solver scope

Bots use only their own hole cards, the public board, public stacks and betting statistics. They cannot read another player's private cards. Human-like think time remains separate from the strategy engine.

Cash heads-up postflop decisions use an online chance-sampled counterfactual regret minimization (CFR) solver. It samples 40 range combinations per player (merging duplicates and retaining the bot's actual combination), constructs a legal betting tree with approximately one-third, three-quarter and 1.25-pot sizes plus eligible short-stack shoves, and allows at most one re-raise. Each action is drawn from the learned average strategy rather than a hard-coded bluff percentage. Search runs for up to 24,000 iterations within an 850 ms compute budget in a worker thread; at most two workers run concurrently. Late, failed, overloaded or insufficiently visited solves fall back to the existing range-based rules. Responses from an obsolete hand/street/turn are discarded.

This is a solver-derived **sampled one-street abstraction**, not a complete Hold'em equilibrium solution. River showdown values use exact hand ranks for the sampled ranges. Flop/turn terminal values estimate equity with 12/16 future-board rollouts and assume no betting on later streets. Range weights still use heuristic public-statistics assumptions. Preflop and three-or-more-player pots retain the positional/range-aware heuristic engine. No licensed commercial solver data or trained full-game blueprint is bundled, and professional-level or unexploitable play is not claimed. Increasing aggression or simulation count alone does not establish GTO strength.

Validation includes legal actions/stack caps, range card blockers, unbeatable-hand behavior, hidden-card independence, deadline fallback and worker event-loop responsiveness. The CFR core is tested against Kuhn poker's known -1/18 equilibrium value and exhaustive best responses in that small game. That benchmark validates the core algorithm; it does not measure exploitability or playing strength in full Hold'em.

Algorithm background: Zinkevich et al., [Regret Minimization in Games with Incomplete Information](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/3306-regret-minimization-in-games-with-incomplete-information); Brown and Sandholm, [Superhuman AI for multiplayer poker](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aay2400). This implementation uses standard signed-regret CFR with regret matching, not CFR+.

It queues a fresh tournament after the current hand, resets seated stacks to the configured tournament bank (default 1,000) and blinds to 5/10, and closes seating after play begins.

The clip pre-decodes when game audio unlocks and retries transient loading errors.

Tournament Blind Levels is above Turn Timer, uses the configured 1–120 minute duration (default 7), and freezes during pause. Hand reveals (both voluntary and forced) are unavailable in tournament mode; required showdown exposure remains unchanged. Tournament settings use opening blind values to avoid multiplying an already-increased blind a second time.

The mode switch is anchored outside the information panel and reads Cash in tournaments. Switching back waits for settlement, preserves stacks, restores the previous cash blinds and starting-bank setting, and reopens seating/reveals. The star toolbar menu ranks current stacks in chips and current big blinds; tied chip leaders share a star. Blind multipliers are defined once in `public/tournament-structure.js` and the complete schedule is published by the server.


## PostgreSQL and learning data foundation

Use Neon Free PostgreSQL (no fixed trial expiry; storage/compute quotas still apply). Create a project near the Render service (US East/Virginia). Copy the pooled connection string into the existing Render service's `DATABASE_URL` environment variable. Keep TLS certificate verification enabled; the server upgrades `sslmode=require` to `sslmode=verify-full`. Do not paste credentials into GitHub, public files or chat. Locally, copy `.env.example` to `.env`, fill it privately, and run `npm run db:check`.

On connection the server creates `poker_hands` and `poker_hand_actions` from `database-schema.sql`. Completed hands are written atomically with validated actions. Records include public boards, numeric results and each bot's own cards at its decisions; human hole cards, names, tokens and connection secrets are not archived. A unique UUID per deal prevents collisions across server restarts; writes are idempotent. The game continues if storage is temporarily unavailable; `/health` reports only `database.configured` and `database.connected`, never credentials. Failed writes are logged by error code only and are not guaranteed to be replayed.

Hands provide an append-only record; persistent human-identity tracking is not enabled. Offline parameter training is described below. The separate bot strategy update adds context-sensitive check-raises, occasional trapping checks, selective donk leads and blocker/draw-based bluffs using observed public behavior. Heads-up CFR still handles ordinary solver decisions. These are heuristics, not a measured guarantee of stronger or GTO play.

## Bounded automatic training

`npm run train:nightly` runs cash training and tournament training sequentially, with 7.5 minutes each (15 minutes total, plus database shutdown/current cash hand). `npm run train:check` runs a short non-promoting pipeline check. Requires Node 22 and the ignored local `.env` containing the existing Neon `DATABASE_URL`. No API/model-training fees or extra paid services are introduced. Only one training process runs at a time. Training does not run on the live Render event loop.

This is parameter search over six bounded multiway check-raise, trapping, donk and bluff frequencies, not neural-network training or a claim of GTO strength. The trainer plays independent cash hands with shuffled decks, proper blinds, legal raises, short all-ins and side pots. Candidates are screened with separate seeds, then tested on held-out duplicate deals, rotating all seats, at 30/60/100/150 BB, against the incumbent plus calling-station, tight and aggressive opponents at 3- and 6-player tables. Opponents and policies only see their own hole cards and public history. Human hole cards are never used. The HU continuation in this offline benchmark uses the heuristic rather than the live bounded CFR search, so results are a limited benchmark, not a guarantee of live performance. Cash heads-up trained frequencies remain at the default policy; the four personalities still vary opening ranges and contextual tactics. Tournament play uses the separate policy and full-tournament evaluator below.

Checkpointed validation accumulates across batches under `.training/`, invalidating on policy-parent or simulation/strategy-code changes. Promotion requires 2,000 complete independent validation blocks, full 700-sample equity estimates, a positive 99% normal-approximation lower confidence bound on paired BB/100 improvement, and no negative mean in any of eight opponent/table-size groups. Incomplete, noisy or regressing candidates never replace the live policy. A run can finish without any improvement. Each cycle ends after one held-out test; the next cycle gets fresh seeds.

Neon stores `poker_training_runs`, immutable `poker_bot_policies`, and one `poker_active_policy` pointer. Reports also remain locally in `.training/<run-id>.json`. Promotion checks the expected parent version in a transaction; a stale run cannot overwrite a newer policy. The server checks the pointer at most every 30 minutes, freezes the selected version for each new hand, and applies approved cash frequency changes only to multiway cash decisions. Tournament policies use a separate pointer. Completed hand records include the policy version. No policy changes mid-hand. Keep prior policy rows to support rollback by updating the active pointer to an existing policy ID.

On this computer, the Windows task `Poker Night Continuous Bot Training` starts a hidden supervisor at sign-in. It repeats bounded 15-minute batches with a one-minute break, or a five-minute retry after failures. The computer must be awake, signed in and online; Codex and Chrome need not remain open. The old 03:00 Codex automation was removed. The machine-local supervisor and its bounded log are under the ignored `.training/` directory. Reports and checkpoints are ignored by Git; the supervisor does not edit source, push commits, change hosting plans or print credentials. Watching the live arena produces recorded matches, but the separate offline evaluation is what decides whether a strategy update qualifies.


### Efficient candidate screening

Both modes try three bounded proposals: a nearby perturbation and wider alternatives. Each is screened on the same 128 low-cost comparison blocks/pairs across opponent pools, styles and stack depths, using 32 equity samples. Baseline simulations are cached within the batch across candidates, keyed by the full screening context. Compact action traces record whether decisions changed, even when final winnings tie. Only candidates that changed decisions and showed positive screening gain proceed to validation. Tied, unchanged and losing screening rounds are saved as `screened_out`; they never approve a policy. Partial screening resumes on the next batch.

Screening and held-out validation use disjoint seed domains. Validation still requires the full 700 equity samples and the unchanged promotion gates above/below. After at least 250 cash blocks or 64 tournament pairs, a candidate may be rejected early if no decisions changed, or if its approximate 99% upper bound is below zero. This is rejection only; there is no partial-run promotion or reduced sample requirement. Finished/rejected candidates get fresh proposals next batch. Changing evaluator code deliberately starts a new checkpoint; old local reports and Neon records remain available.

## Bot personalities and tournament training

Add bot opens a picker for four styles, plus Mixed to randomly choose one. Each bot keeps its chosen style for its table session: TAG (tight-aggressive), LAG (loose-aggressive), FISH (loose-passive beginner) or ROCK (very tight-passive). Click its avatar to see its style in Player statistics. Styles adjust opening ranges, trapping, check-raises and bluff mixes in cash, Bot training and tournament games. They do not grant access to another player’s private cards.

Tournament bots use the same range-based decision path as the offline tournament simulator, including heads-up play; cash heads-up CFR remains unchanged. They read the public blind clock, upcoming big blind, effective stacks and observed calling behavior. In the last minute before an actual increase, threatened short stacks and stacks covering vulnerable opponents can widen positional pressure. Short shoves/re-shoves need appropriate stack depth, blockers/playability and a conservative fold-equity screen. River bluff shoves need a nut blocker, a small remaining stack relative to the pot and a credible betting opportunity. Frequent callers and crowded pots suppress those bluffs. These are bounded heuristics, not an exact ICM or full-game equilibrium solution.

The nightly wrapper also runs `npm run train:tournament`, which searches five bounded tournament-specific frequencies. It simulates single-table winner-take-all tournaments at 3/6 seats and 15/30/60/100 starting BB, carrying stacks between hands with no rebuys, standard side pots, eliminations, dealer rotation and the actual blind schedule. Simulation hand time is an exogenous 35–65 seconds; it does not reproduce the live animation/think delays. A rollout can stop when the queried bot is eliminated because its first-place result and finishing position are then known. Cash all-in run-twice rules are not used in tournament simulation.

Screening and validation use disjoint seeded deals. Validation compares candidate/incumbent on matching seeds and rotates starting seats, depths and all four personalities against incumbent, tight, aggressive and calling-station pools. Adoption requires 512 completed tournament pairs, 700 equity samples per decision, a positive 99% normal-approximation lower confidence bound on first-place win-rate gain, no negative mean in any opponent/table-size or personality group, and no mean finishing-position regression. This is a limited self-play benchmark, not proof of stronger play against every human or a guarantee of improvement. The first-place objective matches this site’s single-champion format; prize-ladder/bubble ICM training is not implemented.

Tournament checkpoints and reports are separate from cash (`.training/tournament-checkpoint.json`). Interrupted pairs do not count. Neon retains versioned reports/policies and `poker_active_tournament_policy`; existing cash policies remain separate in `poker_active_policy`. The server reloads qualified updates at most every 30 minutes and freezes the chosen policy for the entire hand. Completed records include bot style and public tournament timing. No candidate is promoted from a smoke check or an incomplete run. It may take many nightly runs to finish validation, and a finished candidate may be rejected. No immediate win/loss learning occurs after a live hand.

`npm run train:check` smoke-checks both modes without promotion. `npm run train:cash` and `npm run train:tournament` can run each pipeline independently. The local continuous Windows supervisor calls the same bounded `train:nightly` wrapper while the computer is awake and signed in.

## Learning from recorded website matches

The local background trainer now incrementally consumes completed `poker_hands` and `poker_hand_actions`, including older records. Each hand is claimed once in `poker_modelled_hands`; transactional, mode-separated counters in `poker_opponent_models` measure VPIP, PFR, aggression and responses to bets. Only validated public actions count. Human identities remain anonymous table-session IDs, with no cross-session identity matching. The server also updates these completed-hand models immediately in memory, restores persisted counts at most once per minute, and feeds them to its existing range/calling-station/aggression heuristics. Counts from the same session are selected by completed-hand coverage, never added twice. Small-sample thresholds in the poker logic still apply.

A uniform reservoir retains at most four private decision snapshots per completed hand to bound database growth while all ordinary actions remain logged. Each snapshot uses a strict whitelist: the bot's own two cards, currently visible board, stack/contribution/betting state, legal raise state, relative seats, public actions and tournament clock. Opponents' hole cards, future boards, names, tokens and connections are excluded. Snapshots are stored only in server/database archives, never client state. Older incomplete logs contribute opponent tendencies but are not presented as exact decision replays.

Ingestion selects one decision per eligible source hand. A deterministic hand-ID hash permanently separates 75% training from 25% holdout; cash/tournament corpora are separate. Each candidate freezes up to 64 source hands per partition in its ignored local checkpoint. Offline population estimates use training-partition actions only; snapshot model counters are replaced with those estimates to avoid held-out-model leakage. Reports stored in Neon retain hand IDs, counts and results, not duplicate private scenario bodies.

Candidate/incumbent comparisons resume at the recorded bot decision with identical seeded samples of unknown opponents and future cards, preserving unequal stacks, existing contributions, folded players and side-pot eligibility. These are model-based counterfactual estimates: unknown hands are sampled from approximate public-information ranges and opponents continue with the existing heuristics, not their unknowable actual alternative choices. Actual recorded winnings do not label actions as good/bad. TAG/LAG/FISH/ROCK personalities remain fixed.

Cash simulations additionally rotate observed training-population models. In both modes, recorded training scenarios can exclude candidates that regress before ordinary validation. Existing 2,000-block cash and 512-pair full-tournament promotion gates remain unchanged. Once a candidate uses recorded training scenarios, adoption additionally requires positive 99% approximate lower-bound gain on 32 distinct held-out source hands, four paired unknown-card rollouts per hand with 700-sample equity estimates. Replicates are averaged per source hand, not counted as independent hands. Missing evidence, overlap, regression or an outdated parent prevents promotion. Tournament recorded-hand BB tests supplement, and never replace, the full-tournament first-place/placement checks.

Each bounded trainer invocation processes up to 50 pending archived hands, sharing the batch between the oldest backlog and newest records. Frozen evaluation data stays unchanged across checkpoint resumes; newly ingested hands enter a later candidate's corpus. Processing and full evaluation run locally, not on Render. New snapshot scenarios accumulate after deployment; enough held-out hands must be collected before recorded-data candidates can qualify. This is bounded parameter learning plus opponent modelling, not neural-network training, an equilibrium solver or guaranteed improvement after each match.


### Cash-game voluntary public shows

When a human explicitly shows their own cards after a cash hand, bots retain exactly the publicly shown card(s), with the hand number and whether that player raised preflop. A second-card reveal updates the same record; it does not count as another hand. Memory is bounded to the last 12 shown hands per player within the table session and is cleared with the room/server restart. It never treats those old cards as the player's next hand or as blockers in a new deck.

Bot decision views receive this public history for future hands. After at least six complete shown preflop raises, both heuristic range sampling and heads-up solver range assumptions apply a small capped adjustment from their observed starting-hand quality. Voluntary shows are selective evidence, so this is deliberately weak and is not a measured bluff-rate estimate. Snapshot models retain only the numeric public-read summary, never another player's raw past cards. Private Reveal hand/Reveal all bot hands never enter this memory, and tournament behaviour is unchanged. Automatic showdown exposure is separate from this explicit self-show feature.


All-in music reads the same in-memory, per-tab mute state as ordinary game effects. The saved `poker-muted` preference initializes new/reloaded tabs, but changing it in a different tab cannot silently mute the theme while this tab's sound button and other effects remain on. Effects and theme scripts are cache-versioned together.


## Google accounts and bankroll Cash

Custom is the original editable cash-style game (`MAIN` internally remains mode `cash`). Bankroll Cash uses fixed shared `CASH1` ($1/$2, $40–$200 buy-in) and `CASH2` ($5/$10, $200–$1,000 buy-in) tables. Two connected funded players start automatically. Custom and tournament chips cannot enter wallets. Bankroll Cash does not allow bots or editable rules.

Set server-only `DATABASE_URL` and the public `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` in ignored `.env` locally or Render Environment. Configure a Google Identity Services Web client with the exact deployed origin and any local test origin (currently `http://localhost:3191`; use `PORT=3191`). No client secret is needed. Google basic identity only is used; the server validates Google's signed ID token, client audience and CSRF nonce. The opaque account cookie is HttpOnly, Secure over HTTPS and expires after 30 days.

`bankroll-schema.sql` creates separate account, session, wallet, seat, table-lease, settlement and ledger tables. Each verified Google subject receives 5,000 play chips once. One funded Cash seat per account, atomic buy-ins/top-ups/cash-outs and conserved server-authored settlements prevent importing client balances. Cash out between hands; leaving mid-hand folds a non-all-in hand and returns the remaining settled stack afterward. An all-in leaver retains pot entitlement. Reconnecting the same account resumes its funded seat without a second debit.

One server instance owns each Cash table with a 60-second renewable lease. After a crashed instance's lease expires, the next process voids an unfinished hand and refunds its last settled pre-hand stacks exactly once. Completed hands remain settled. Database/lease failures freeze Cash play and payouts for retry rather than accepting unrecorded money changes. These remain virtual play chips, not redeemable currency.

Verification: `node --test --test-concurrency=2` includes account/CSRF, wallet, reconnect, mode isolation and three-player side-pot/run-it-twice settlement tests. `test-support/cash-loader.mjs` is an explicit isolated test-process loader that replaces Google verification and PostgreSQL with fixtures; never use that loader with `npm start` or deployment. Daily bonuses, refills, XP and reward quests are future features.
